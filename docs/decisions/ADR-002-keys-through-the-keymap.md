# ADR-002: Keys reach the keymap through riff's own entry point

## Status
Accepted

## Context
riff moves its keys from the priority chain in `src/app/global-keys.ts` into `@opentui/keymap` layers ([spec 100](../../specs/100-solid-migration.md), M6), as tenor already holds them. The chain has 30-odd branches, and they move one or a few at a time, each step shipping on its own; for the length of the migration both dispatchers are live.

riff records navigation in one place: every keypress passes through the jump recorder ([spec 089](../../specs/089-jump-history.md)), which captures where the cursor was before an interaction and judges whether the interaction has settled, a pending chord included. Anything that handles a key outside that call escapes the jumplist.

The stock OpenTUI adapter (`createOpenTuiKeymapHost`) subscribes the keymap itself, with `prependListener` on `renderer.keyInput`.

| Approach | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Stock adapter.** The keymap listens on `keyInput` itself, ahead of riff's listener. | <ul><li>No host code of riff's own</li><li>tenor's setup unchanged</li></ul> | <ul><li>Keymap commands run outside the jump recorder: a navigation moved into a layer leaves the jumplist</li><li>A key the keymap took still reaches the chain unless every branch checks `defaultPrevented`</li><li>Two entry points for keys</li></ul> |
| **B. A host riff feeds.** The OpenTUI host in every respect but `onKeyPress`, which stores the listener; riff's one keypress listener offers each key to it and passes what it leaves to the chain. | <ul><li>One entry point, inside the jump recorder</li><li>The chain sees only what the keymap did not take, by construction</li><li>A pending sequence is visible to the recorder as a chord</li></ul> | <ul><li>A small host wrapper riff maintains against the keymap's `KeymapHost` interface</li></ul> |
| **C. Port the whole chain in one step.** | <ul><li>No period with two dispatchers</li></ul> | <ul><li>One unreviewable change across every feature's keys; a regression cannot be pinned to a branch</li></ul> |

## Decision
B. The jump recorder is riff's single entry point for keys, and moving branches one at a time needs the chain to see only what the keymap leaves. `createRiffKeymap` (`src/app/keymap.ts`) builds the keymap on that host with the addons `createDefaultOpenTuiKeymap` installs, and `composeKeys` (`src/app/keys-dispatch.ts`) puts it in front of the chain. Only `onKeyPress` goes through that entry point: `onKeyRelease` and `onRawInput` stay on the stock OpenTUI host, so a release binding or a raw intercept would run outside the jump recorder.

## Consequences
- Every binding in the keymap outranks every branch still in the chain. Branches move top-down in chain order, or the step that moves one out of order proves the two can never be active together.
- A handled binding calls `preventDefault`, so a focused input or textarea does not also receive that key. Where a chain branch swallowed a key without `preventDefault`, moving it can change what a focused field sees, and the step that moves it checks that. A key that breaks off a pending sequence is neither prevented nor stopped: on a sequence miss the keymap drops the pending sequence and reports the key unhandled, and a sequence binding whose command returns false is rejected the same way, so `offer()` returns false and the key goes on as it arrived. A key that a capture holds drops the pending sequence too, when its layer stands aside under captures, and goes to the capture.
- A key that breaks off a keymap sequence is offered to the layers again through `reoffer()`, with the chord still counting as pending, before it reaches the leftover as the second key of a chord: the keymap does not dispatch a missed key from the root, and the chain asked the chat and its modal branches ahead of its sequences while its global keys declined under a chord. Every layer that gives way to a chord reads the composed chord and declines it again; the chat's reach, which ignores chords, takes Ctrl-r. A key the layers leave is the second key of a chord to the leftover, as it was to the chain when the chain held the opener: `composeKeys` reads the keymap's pending sequence before the offer and calls the leftover with `chordBroken`, so the diff layer's split toggle stands aside under a chord, the diff layer takes the key at the re-offer, and the leftover hands it to the tree. An opener that breaks off a keymap sequence (the second `]` of `]]f`, the `]` of `g]f`, the second `z` of `gzz`) opens its own at the re-offer, as the bracket, `z` and `g` layers read no pending chord; a mark opener does not (the `'` of `]'`), as its pattern reads `reoffering()` and the chain opened no mark chord on a chord's second key; a capture that leaves the first key and stands aside under a chord (the help overlay's bare `g`) stands aside at the re-offer too, so `g]f` and `gzz` act behind the help. Every key that breaks off a sequence is re-offered, so a layer that ignores chords acts on it: F12 toggles the debug console after a lone `]`, `[`, `z` or `g`, as it did under the chain's `g`. Under a peek, which leaves only a bare `g`, the `g` layer binds every second key the peek refuses, so it is swallowed rather than broken off and handed to the diff, as the chain swallowed it; it leaves a key the chat or a layer that ignores chords takes (Ctrl-r, F12), which breaks off the sequence and acts at the re-offer. The keymap has no timeout of its own on a pending sequence, so a layer whose chain chord expired arms one on the `pendingSequence` event and calls `clearPendingSequence()`, as `registerTimedLeader` does, and the mark chords, which wait as long as it takes, arm none; the sequence layers share one such listener (`src/app/sequence-expiry.ts`) with the chain's `chordTimeoutMs`, as each pending sequence replaces the last and two listeners would disarm each other's timers. A second key that ignores modifiers, as the chain's chords did, is a sequence pattern matching on the chain's suffix (`src/app/chord-stroke.ts`), since a plain stroke compares every modifier, and which matches no key the chat reaches, since the chain asked the chat ahead of its chord matcher: Ctrl-r after `]` misses the sequence and reaches the chat at the re-offer rather than ending `]r`. Its token marks shift and a capital explicitly, as token names fold to lower case, and `createAppKeys` registers each pattern once for every sequence layer, as the keymap refuses a name registered twice. The same folding lets a binding on a letter take the key named by its capital without shift, so an opener that must not (the `z` of the `z` chords, the `g` of the `g` chords) is a sequence pattern too, and so is an opener that opens only in some states (the `m` and `'` of the mark chords, which need the diff to have the keys). A pending mark chord whose diff has lost the keys, say to a click into the tree, gives way before any layer sees the next key: `composeKeys` runs the mark layer's `settle` ahead of the offer, which clears the pending sequence, so the key reaches every layer as one with no chord pending.
- The keymap matches modifiers exactly where the chain matched a key by name, so a layer that takes over a branch which ignored modifiers binds every ctrl, shift, meta, super and hyper variant of its keys; alt and option arrive as meta (`anyModifiers`, `src/app/key-variants.ts`).
- A modal that captures every key (flash, the confirm dialog) registers `capture` next to its layer, so the chain never sees a key while that modal is active. A capture does not prevent the keys it keeps, so a modal whose focused field types what it leaves unbound (the command palette) captures without a `rest` handler and its field still receives them. Of two enabled layers binding a key, and of two active captures, the later registered wins.
- A capture decides per key: `active` receives the key, so a modal can leave one to the layers behind it (the help overlay's bare `g`, which `g?` needs). A layer that must yield to a pending chord reads the chain view `createAppKeys` builds, which is the keymap's pending sequence: every chord the chain asked (the bracket motions, the `z`, `g` and mark chords) is a keymap sequence. The sequence layers read none, as a pending sequence holds the next key itself. Every modal layer is enabled by its capture's `holds()`, plus any condition of its own, and so runs only while that capture is the one holding the key: it stands aside under every modal that outranks it, even for keys that modal leaves unbound. The help overlay is where this shows today, since the pickers and Submit Review that its `g`-chords open over it type its j, k and q; enabled by their own state alone, the palette and the pickers would run the keys the confirmation dialog leaves unbound, and the dialog would take y and n from flash drawn over it. `holds()` is true only while `offer()` feeds a key, so a keymap query outside dispatch (an active-keys list, a which-key display, a palette catalog filtered by enabled) sees these layers as disabled; whoever builds such a view has to account for that.
- A layer that must answer outside every modal and inside its own, such as the chat's Ctrl-r, is enabled by `!held() || captured.holds()`: `held()` says whether any capture holds the key being offered. It has the reverse caveat: `held()` is false outside `offer()`, so a keymap query outside dispatch sees such a layer enabled even under a modal.
- A layer's `enabled` must not call `RiffKeymap.chordPending()`: the keymap evaluates `enabled` while it checks its own pending sequence, and the call recurses into that check. A layer that gives way to a keymap sequence reads it in `run` and returns false there; the sequence layers' `enabled` reads only `held()`, and a pattern that must tell a re-offered key reads `reoffering()`, which never asks the keymap.
- `composeKeys`' `chordPending` is the keymap's pending sequence, or a key that broke one off going round the layers again, so the jump recorder judges a chord as one interaction: `'a` is recorded from where `'` was pressed.
- The chain is retired: every branch is a layer, and what the layers leave goes to the tree's leftover (`treeLeftover` in `src/features/file-tree/keys.ts`), which hands the tree a key that broke off a chord or a slash kitty names after its base key.
- The palette and help keep their display-only `shortcut` strings until the commands they name live in the keymap; `getCommandEntries` becomes the catalog then, as in tenor's `src/app/registry.ts`.
